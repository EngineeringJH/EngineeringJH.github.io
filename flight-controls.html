The Problem: The Limits of Spring-Loaded Sticks

Most consumer flight controls (HOTAS) are passive and spring-centered: they pull back to equilibrium regardless of flight condition. In actual flight, control loading changes dynamically with airspeed, dynamic pressure, and trim. During an aerodynamic stall, a spring stick communicates zero physical feedback, forcing the pilot to rely entirely on visual instruments. My goal is to convert a spring-centered HOTAS to a dynamically controlled one.

Engineering Approach & Cost Strategy

Commercial active force-feedback bases range from $600 to over $2,000 while outputting only 6–12 N of torque. This design targets 45 N peak output at a sub-$200 build cost through:

  • Brushless DC (BLDC) Direct Drive: Utilizing harvested high-pole-count BLDC motors for smooth torque delivery with zero belt slack or gear backlash.
  • Custom Gimbal Architecture: Designed from scratch in Siemens NX to fit high-rigidity CNC aluminum bearing blocks and 3D-printed housing tolerances.
  • Open-Source Integration: Interfacing with the FFBeast motor controller firmware for real-time telemetry extraction from Microsoft Flight Simulator and DCS World.

Hardware Specifications

  • Materials: 6061-T6 Aluminum (bearing blocks & critical load paths), PETG (structural enclosure)
  • Actuators: Dual 500W BLDC motors (Pitch & Roll axes)
  • Peak Theoretical Force: 45 N at full deflection
